Both are possible — it's a real fork, not a forced cut. The trade:

**Rigid parts (current)** — cut at joint boundaries, each part is a whole-instance matrix. Cheapest possible render (one instanced draw per part for the *entire swarm*), but seams show at joint angles and every creature shares the same pose phase unless we fake it per-part.

**Bendable attached mesh (skinning)** — one mesh, bones + vertex weights, vertices blend across joints → genuinely organic bends, real elbow/knee/wrist deformation, wing membranes that *stretch*. Three options to render it:

1. **SkinnedMesh per creature** — the honest path. One draw call per rakk. At ~10–30 rakks × ~3k tris, trivial for the GPU. We lose swarm instancing for that species but keep the `SwarmPart` architecture for everything else. Blender rig → GLTF with skinning → three.js `Skeleton` — this is the standard path, zero engine work needed, and it unlocks *real* wing folding, tail lashing, jaw flex.

2. **Instanced skinning (shader trick)** — keep ONE draw call: add `skinIndex`/`skinWeight` vertex attributes, stuff per-instance bone matrices into an `InstancedBufferAttribute`/texture, do linear-blend-skinning in `onBeforeCompile`. three.js doesn't ship this, it's a known community pattern (~100 lines of shader patch). Best of both worlds: whole swarm skinned, still instanced. Cost: a custom material to maintain.

3. **Morph/baked keyframes** — bake N poses into morph targets, lerp per-instance. Cheap but pose count is fixed and blending wings mid-flap between two bakes looks mushy for limbs.

**My take**: rakks spawn in single digits to low dozens — a SkinnedMesh each is a non-issue performance-wise, and it's the only path where the wing membrane *billows* instead of hinging. The Blender pipeline writes skin weights that survive GLB export. Instanced skinning is the "scale it later" card if we ever want 200+ flying creatures.

So the workflow wouldn't change much: Blender sculpt → auto-rig or hand rig (7–10 bones: spine chain, 2 wing chains, tail chain, jaw) → skin weights → export GLB → `SkinnedMesh` clone per rakk, drive bones from `animT`/`state`. The current instanced version stays as the swarm fallback — genuinely useful to keep both paths since `SpeciesRenderer` is a shared constraint.
