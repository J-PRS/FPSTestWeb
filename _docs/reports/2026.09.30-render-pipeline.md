Short version: **not universal (~85% support as of now), and for us it'd be a big port for uncertain gain** — our bottleneck is CPU-side scene graph overhead, which WebGPU doesn't fix.

## Support status

- **Chrome/Edge**: solid since 113, ~95%+ of their install base
- **Safari**: only landed in **Safari 26** (Sep 2025) — anyone on iOS/macOS not updated this year gets nothing
- **Firefox**: **141** (July 2025, Windows), macOS only at 145, **Linux not at all**
- caniuse composite: ~86% global — **1 in 7 players gets nothing**
- Mitigant: `WebGPURenderer` has a built-in **WebGL2 fallback backend** — you write TSL once and it compiles to GLSL when WebGPU is absent. So "support" isn't really the blocker.

## What it would actually get us

- **Compute shaders** — the real prize: particles simmed entirely GPU-side (like the Niagara-style libs I mentioned). But quarks on WebGL already batches to one draw call, so for our *particle counts* it's headroom we don't yet need.
- Lower CPU overhead per draw call (bundle encoding vs per-call GL state checks) — helps the draw-call-bound problem we actually have.

## The real downsides for this codebase

1. **Postprocessing is the killer.** We use `postprocessing` (the pmndrs lib) — it's **WebGL-only**, no WebGPU backend, and the maintainers have said a WebGPU version isn't planned. We'd have to port bloom/contrast/upscale/composer to three's TSL post-processing or write effects by hand. Weeks of work, visual drift guaranteed during the transition.
2. **Every custom GLSL shader needs rewriting in TSL** — ACES grade, upscale filter, the shield-bubble hex/fresnel material, blob shadows, the terrain material if customized. TSL is JS-typed shader nodes, not a text port — it's a re-authoring pass.
3. **`renderer.info`-style introspection differs**; perf HUD and the ppdebug instrumentation would need rework.
4. **Quarks** supports WebGPU only via its newer WGSL variant — our just-landed integration would need re-verification.
5. Maturity edge cases: WebGPURenderer still has feature gaps (MSAA on some paths, certain texture formats, more aggressive shader compilation hitching on first frame).

## Recommendation

Stay on `WebGLRenderer` + quarks. The honest reading of our own perf dumps: the fixable costs are **draw-call batching + particle CPU updates**, both addressed by quarks/instancing under WebGL2. Port to WebGPU later *if* we want GPU-compute particles at 50k+ scale — and by then Safari/Firefox support will be deeper too. The 85% support + postprocessing rewrite makes it a poor trade right now.

## Common-sense non-particle wins worth knowing

- **`three-mesh-bvh`** — if we ever want particles to *collide* with terrain cheaply, or faster raycasts for hitscan/debris.
- **`postprocessing` we already have** — its `GodRaysEffect`/`SSAO`/custom `Effect` slots are where stylized glows live without more particle work.
- **Sprite-sheet flipbooks** (quarks supports `uvTileSheet` natively) — animated smoke/fire texture atlases get AAA-looking blasts at quad cost. Cheapest visual-quality upgrade per ms.
- **`troika-three-text`** — if damage numbers ever hurt, it's the standard SDF-text batcher.

Stick with quarks — it's the boring-correct choice and we've already paid the integration cost.
