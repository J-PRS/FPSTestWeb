# One sim clock — BallSim/CreatureSim onto room.sims (2026-10-01)

## What was wrong

`room.sims` (the ROADMAP lifecycle registry) existed, and TickLoop steps
every entry at phase 0-after-commands — but `BallSim` and `CreatureSim`,
the two *world* sims, still ticked on their own private `setInterval`s.
Three unsynchronized clocks in one room:

- ball/creature decisions interleaved arbitrarily with the command drain
  and moveSim — the ordering we fixed for messages never applied to
  world state;
- each sim did its own wall-clock catch-up (the code's own comment
  measured setInterval firing +11ms late → ~18% sim-time drift),
  so balls, creatures and players each ran slightly different clocks.

## Pattern

One tick updates everything (Quake/Valve single simulation frame —
confirmed again in current fixed-timestep literature: a central loop
owns all sims; nothing inside the sim owns a clock). The registry the
roadmap already prescribed was the right shape — the fix was removing
the two sims' self-drive, not inventing a new one.

## Landed

- `BallSim`/`CreatureSim`: `setInterval`, `tick()` and `lastTickAt`
  deleted; `attachField` no longer starts a clock. The public `step(dt)`
  (already used by scenario tests) keeps whole-cell catch-up inside, so
  a late tick still lands whole 50ms cells.
- `Room.registerSims` pushes `ballSim.step` + `creatureSim.step` first —
  wildlife steps before bots and downstream systems, so every consumer
  sees post-step positions in the same tick.
- `stop()` (teardown call site) now just resets the accumulator; no
  clock to clear.
- `tests/scenarios/harness.ts` dropped its two manual sim-step calls —
  room.sims now covers them, so tests drive the exact production order.

## Verify

- `bunx tsc --noEmit`: clean.
- `bun test`: 736/736 pass — including netbot real-socket suites and all
  ball/creature parity scenarios, now ticking through the room loop.
