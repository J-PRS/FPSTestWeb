## Cheap techniques the industry actually uses

**1. Verlet ragdoll (the original one)** — Hitman: Codename 47 was built on this (Jakobsen's method). No physics engine: ~16 point masses connected by distance constraints ("sticks" for bones, "limits" for joint angle stops), gravity integrated from position deltas, ground collision = clamp y. 3D versions are the same math — a few hundred lines of code, zero dependencies. The ragdollify project does 16-joint dummies at 120Hz with 8 relaxation passes in a single HTML file. **This is the sweet spot for you**: real flopping limbs, no wasm dep, tiny CPU. Effort: medium — bone mapping per rig + a solver + pose write-back into `SkeletonUtils` bones.

**2. Recorded ragdoll deaths (Drunken Monday trick)** — they hit the same problem on mobile, and solved it by *recording* a few real ragdoll deaths as animation clips, then playing a random one per kill. The multiplayer kicker is clever: the server sends the impact vector, and the client picks the clip whose recorded vector is closest. You already have blast direction on the wire (`launchCorpse` uses it), and your rigs are GLB with real clips. Runtime cost ≈ one clip play. Cost is the one-time bake tooling.

**3. Hybrid anim→limp (half-and-half)** — play a short death clip, then hand off to ragdoll, or use "powered" spring joints that track the animation but yield to physics. Diablo 3's partial ragdoll (GDC paper) and UE's update-joints-from-animation trick. Eliminates the "sack of potatoes" drop while keeping physics cheap. Pairs with option 1: a Verlet corpse can play 0.5s of death clip before going limp.

**4. Partial ragdoll** — simulate only head/arms/spine, keep legs animated or frozen. Godot and UE both support per-bone toggles. Cuts the per-corpse cost in half.

**5. Gibs + corpse hybrid (what you want)** — the Unity "Gore Simulator" approach: corpse bursts into loose parts *and* each part gets its own tumble. You already have the parts side — `PlayerDebris` gibs tumble with `inheritVel`. The missing half is that a gib burst should come *out of* a launched corpse, not replace it.

## Real costs everyone pays

- **Freeze corpses fast.** Every engine does this — freeze the sim after ~2s of settling, or the corpse just keeps burning CPU on a hill. Your `stepCorpse` already shrinks/despawns, which is the same idea.
- **LOD / distance gating.** Skip per-bone updates for far corpses, or drop to a coarse skeleton.
- **Bone pruning.** Drop finger/hand bones — nobody reads them during a death.

## What I'd actually build for this game

Start with option 5 = wire what you have (`launchCorpse` + `PlayerDebris` on every death). Then option 1 (Verlet limbs) if the corpse still reads stiff, or option 2 (recorded deaths) if you want "real" look without solver tuning. Rapier/wasm is the last resort — real physics, worst payoff.
