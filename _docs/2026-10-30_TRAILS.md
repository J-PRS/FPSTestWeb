Researched — the smear-between-teleports is a solved problem, and the industry fix is **not** guarding pushes (you've been fighting the guard for weeks because every caller has to remember to cut). It's making the *geometry itself* incapable of painting the chord:

**1. Degenerate-triangle stitch break** (the standard GPU trick, what Unreal/Babylon/three-gmd do): instead of trusting callers to `clear()` before a jump, when a slot is claimed or a discontinuity is detected you write a **collapsed segment** — stamp both edge verts of the boundary quad to the same position, or width-0. The strip still "connects" old→new, but the connecting triangles have **zero area → zero pixels**. The smear literally cannot render, no matter which caller forgot to cut.

**2. The Babylon answer** (their PR #15369, literally your problem): they added an explicit `reset()` because pooled trail state painting across a teleport was un-fixable by convention — same conclusion: *the buffer must reset itself, callers can't be trusted.*

**3. Unity's answer** is the crude version: `emitting=false`/`Clear()` per call site — which is exactly the convention you've been fighting for weeks, and why it keeps leaking through a new path.

Applied to your batch: on `claim()` and on `clear()`, stamp the slot's ring with a degenerate pair at the spawn pos (all `px/py/pz` = same point, `count=0`). Then even a stale-owner push or a re-pinned slot renders zero-area tris until real samples arrive. The jump guards you have stay as the *diagnostic* layer (`topGaps` proved pushes aren't the source); the degenerate stamp is the *correctness* layer.
